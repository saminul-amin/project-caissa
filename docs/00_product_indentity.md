# Caissa — Product Identity and Philosophy

**Document Type:** Product Identity Brief
**Version:** 1.0

# 1. Product Name

## Caissa

Caissa is a modern, AI-powered chess experience built around human-like play, thoughtful learning, and elegant interaction.

The name is inspired by Caïssa, the traditional poetic figure associated with chess. For product branding, the simplified spelling **Caissa** should be preferred because it is easier to type, search, pronounce, and use in URLs and source code.

### Recommended Usage

* Product name: **Caissa**
* Repository name: `caissa`
* Application title: **Caissa**
* Internal project identifier: `project-caissa`

---

# 2. Primary Slogan

> **Beyond the Best Move**

This slogan represents the central philosophy of Caissa.

Traditional chess engines focus on identifying the objectively strongest move. Caissa goes further by helping players understand:

* Why humans choose particular moves
* Which mistakes are realistic at different skill levels
* How decisions develop during a game
* How players can improve without being overwhelmed by engine calculations

The product is not opposed to engine analysis. It uses engine strength selectively while placing human understanding at the center of the experience.

---

# 3. Supporting Tagline

> **Human-like chess. Meaningful improvement.**

This tagline may be used in application metadata, promotional materials, store descriptions, social posts, and the itch.io project page.

---

# 4. Core Belief

Chess is not only a search for perfect moves.

It is a human game shaped by uncertainty, pressure, pattern recognition, intuition, confidence, mistakes, and learning.

Most chess software explains games from the perspective of an almost-perfect machine. Caissa should explain chess from the perspective of the person playing it.

---

# 5. Product Philosophy

Caissa believes that chess improvement should feel like discovery rather than judgment.

Players should not finish a game only knowing that the engine disliked their moves. They should understand:

* What they were likely trying to achieve
* Why a move was tempting
* What danger they overlooked
* What a stronger player might have noticed
* Which lesson can be carried into the next game

Caissa should transform analysis from a list of numerical evaluations into a clear learning experience.

---

# 6. Vision

> Create the most thoughtful and beautifully designed human-centered chess experience.

Caissa should become a place where players can enjoy chess, face believable opponents, understand their decisions, and improve naturally.

---

# 7. Mission

Build a premium chess platform that combines:

* Human-like artificial intelligence
* Reliable engine analysis
* Clear educational feedback
* Elegant visual design
* Smooth and satisfying interaction
* Respect for the player’s attention

---

# 8. Product Promise

Every Caissa experience should help the player do at least one of the following:

1. Enjoy the game
2. Understand a decision
3. Recognize a pattern
4. Learn from a mistake
5. Feel motivated to play again

A feature that contributes to none of these outcomes should not be prioritized.

---

# 9. Market Positioning

Caissa is not intended to be:

* A clone of Chess.com
* A replacement interface for Stockfish
* A feature-heavy chess portal
* A competitive online chess network at launch
* A collection of disconnected chess utilities

Caissa is positioned as:

> A premium, human-centered chess application where realistic AI opponents and understandable analysis help players enjoy and improve at chess.

---

# 10. Primary Differentiator

Most chess applications ask:

> What was the best move?

Caissa should also ask:

> Why did the player make this move, and what can they learn from it?

This difference should influence every major feature.

# 11. Experience Principles

## 11.1 Human Before Engine

Engine evaluations should support understanding rather than dominate the interface.

Raw centipawn values, long variations, and technical information should be available when useful, but they should not be the default language of the product.

---

## 11.2 Clarity Before Complexity

Caissa should explain one important idea clearly rather than display ten pieces of information simultaneously.

The interface should reveal additional depth progressively.

---

## 11.3 Guidance Without Judgment

The product should never make the player feel unintelligent.

Avoid language such as:

* Terrible move
* Obvious mistake
* You completely failed
* Easy move missed

Prefer language such as:

* This move allowed your opponent to create a strong threat.
* You had a safer option here.
* This idea was natural, but it overlooked the exposed king.
* Consider checking your opponent’s forcing moves first.

---

## 11.4 Realistic Opposition

AI opponents should feel believable.

Different opponents may demonstrate:

* Different rating levels
* Tactical strengths and weaknesses
* Positional preferences
* Risk tolerance
* Time-pressure behavior
* Realistic inaccuracies

The goal is not merely to weaken a strong engine. It is to model recognizable human decision-making.

---

## 11.5 Beauty With Purpose

Visual quality is a core product feature.

Animations, typography, sound, spacing, lighting, and transitions should make the experience feel calm and premium. However, visual effects must never interfere with board readability or gameplay.

---

## 11.6 Respect for Attention

Caissa should avoid:

* Unnecessary notifications
* Cluttered dashboards
* Manipulative engagement patterns
* Distracting advertisements
* Excessive pop-ups
* Artificial urgency

The product should invite users to return because the experience is valuable.

---

## 11.7 Reflection After Play

The end of a game should not feel like the end of the experience.

Players should be guided naturally from:

```text
Play
  ↓
Result
  ↓
Reflection
  ↓
Key lesson
  ↓
Optional deeper analysis
  ↓
Play again
```

# 14. Brand Voice

The Caissa voice should be concise, respectful, and insightful.

## Preferred Voice

> Your kingside attack had potential, but this move allowed Black to challenge the center first.

> This was a natural developing move. A stronger continuation was available because the knight was temporarily undefended.

> You played this phase well. The key improvement is to check forcing moves before beginning the attack.

## Avoided Voice

> Bad move.

> Huge blunder!

> Stockfish says this is losing.

> You should have easily found the best move.

# 17. Feature Evaluation Framework

Before accepting a major feature, the team should answer:

1. Does this support the core philosophy?
2. Does it help users play, understand, or improve?
3. Could it make the experience unnecessarily complex?
4. Can it be introduced progressively?
5. Does it differentiate Caissa from ordinary chess applications?
6. Can it meet the expected visual and interaction quality?
7. Is it important for the current release?

A feature should not be added merely because another chess platform has it.

---

# 18. Product Decision Hierarchy

When requirements conflict, decisions should follow this order:

1. Correctness of chess rules
2. Clarity of user experience
3. Player learning value
4. Responsiveness and performance
5. Visual elegance
6. Feature quantity

A smaller polished product is preferable to a larger inconsistent one.

* Human versus computer mode

* Timers
* Game completion flow
* Basic post-game review

* Advanced game review
* Human move prediction
* Personalized coaching
* Opening insights
* Player statistics
* Puzzles
* Accounts and synchronization

# 20. Identity Statement

Caissa is a human-centered chess experience for players who want more than an engine telling them the best move.

It combines realistic AI play, thoughtful analysis, and premium design to help players understand chess more naturally.

Its defining belief is simple:

> **The best chess experience is not only about finding the best move. It is about understanding the player who must make it.**
